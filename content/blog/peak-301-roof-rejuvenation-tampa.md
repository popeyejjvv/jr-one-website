# Peak 301 Roof Rejuvenation: What Tampa Homeowners Need to Know

If your asphalt shingle roof is 8-20 years old and starting to show its age — curling edges, granule loss, dark streaks — you've probably started thinking about the cost of a full roof replacement. In Tampa, that's $8,000 to $25,000+ depending on your roof size.

Peak 301 offers a different approach: instead of replacing the roof, rejuvenate the existing shingles at a fraction of the cost. Here's how it works and whether it makes sense for your home.

## How Peak 301 Works

Asphalt shingles are made with petroleum-based oils that keep the material flexible and weather-resistant. Over time — typically 8-15 years in Florida's intense UV environment — those oils evaporate. The shingles dry out, become brittle, and start failing: curling at the edges, losing granules, cracking under thermal cycling.

Peak 301 is a soy-based bio-oil that's sprayed onto the roof surface. It penetrates into the shingle material and replaces the lost petroleum oils, restoring flexibility and weather resistance.

It's not a coating that sits on top. It's not a sealant. It's a material-level restoration of the shingle's original properties.

## What Peak 301 Does

- **Restores shingle flexibility** — reduces curling and cracking
- **Slows granule loss** — the protective surface layer stays intact longer
- **Extends roof life by 5-15 years** — depending on current condition and remaining useful life
- **Improves wind resistance** — flexible shingles hold up better in storms than brittle ones
- **Comes with a warranty** — coverage varies by application (need to see your specific roof)

## What Peak 301 Does NOT Do

- **Fix structural damage** — rotten decking, damaged underlayment, or missing shingles need actual repair
- **Work on non-shingle roofs** — tile, metal, flat/membrane, and slate roofs are not candidates
- **Replace a roof that's already failed** — if shingles are severely deteriorated, missing, or leaking, the roof needs replacement, not rejuvenation
- **Prevent all future issues** — it extends life, it doesn't make the roof immortal

## Is Your Roof a Good Candidate?

**Good candidates:**
- Shingle roofs aged 8-18 years
- Early signs of aging (minor curling, some granule loss, slight brittleness)
- No active leaks or structural damage
- Homeowner wants to extend roof life rather than replace now

**Not good candidates:**
- Roofs already leaking
- Severely deteriorated shingles (large bare patches, significant curling)
- Roofs over 20 years old with extensive wear
- Non-shingle roof types (tile, metal, flat)
