## Typical Storm-Driven Damage Claims

### Hurricane and Tropical Storm
- Gutters ripped off by wind uplift
- Sections of fascia torn away from the roof edge
- Soffit panels missing, dislodged, or pest-compromised
- Siding panels stripped, cracked, or holed by debris
- Downspouts bent, crushed, or separated from gutters
- Roof-edge aluminum trim displaced

### Hail Damage
- Impact dents in aluminum gutters, soffit, and siding
- Granule impact damage visible on fascia wraps
- Cracks in vinyl siding or soffit panels
- Punctures in lightweight gutter material

### Falling Debris
- Tree limb or branch damage to gutter runs
- Impact damage to soffit from airborne debris
- Torn fascia from impact events

## Common Claim Pitfalls and How JR One Helps Avoid Them

**"The claim got denied because the adjuster said it was pre-existing wear."**
Solution: Documentation of storm-driven cause vs age-related wear. JR One's assessment distinguishes between the two with specialist authority.

**"The insurance estimate doesn't cover enough to actually fix the damage."**
Solution: Supplement request backed by itemized scope-of-work and line-item cost justification.

**"The adjuster only paid for partial repair, but code requires full replacement."**
Solution: Code-upgrade documentation. In some cases, Florida building code and your policy's ordinance-or-law coverage can require a repair to be done as a full replacement. We document the code-driven scope so your adjuster can evaluate it; your carrier and code official make the final determination.

**"Contractor said they'd do the repair for the deductible and file the claim themselves."**
Warning: That's insurance fraud. JR One Aluminum does not participate in schemes that waive deductibles or inflate claims. We invoice the homeowner directly, the homeowner files with their carrier, and the carrier pays the claim per policy terms.

## Pre-Storm and Post-Storm Recommendations

### Before a Named Storm
- Photograph existing gutters, soffit, fascia, and siding from every angle and store the images somewhere cloud-based
- Document roof condition with date-stamped photos
- Save receipts from recent aluminum work - proof of condition
- Secure loose items that could become airborne debris

### After a Named Storm
- Document damage with photos before touching anything
- File the claim quickly - Florida carriers have deadlines
- Don't sign anything a door-knocking contractor puts in front of you
- Call a specialist for the damage assessment, not a roofer who "also does gutters"
- Keep detailed records of every cost incurred (tarps, boarding, cleanup)
