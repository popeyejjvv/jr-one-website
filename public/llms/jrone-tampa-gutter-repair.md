## Why Gutter Repair Matters in Tampa
Tampa gets about 51 inches of rain a year packed into short intense events. A gutter that leaks, sags, or has the wrong pitch does not just look bad - it sends thousands of gallons in the wrong place. Water behind the fascia rots wood fast in Tampa's humidity. Water at the foundation erodes soil and can crack slab edges. Tampa's UV also breaks down the old silicone seals at miters and downspout outlets faster than inland climates. Most Tampa gutter systems need at least a minor repair every few years.

## FAQ
**Q: My gutter overflows in the middle of a run. Is that a clog or a repair?**
A: Start with cleaning. If overflow continues after the channel and downspout are clear, it's almost always a pitch problem, a sagging hanger, or 5-inch gutters too small for the roof area.

**Q: The old spike-and-ferrule hangers keep pulling out - can you just put them back in?**
A: Technically yes, long-term no. In Tampa heat and humidity the wood holes widen and the spikes keep pulling out. The real fix is switching to hidden hangers screwed into the fascia.

**Q: I've got rot behind the gutter. Do you fix that too?**
A: Yes. JR One replaces fascia and soffit as part of gutter repair when the wood behind the system has rotted. Common on South Tampa and Seminole Heights homes 25+ years old.

**Q: How soon can you get out for a repair?**
A: Most Tampa repair diagnostics within 3 to 7 days. Post-hurricane response is same or next day for existing customers.

**Q: Is it worth repairing or should I replace?**
A: We'll tell you honestly. Gutters under 15 years old with a localized issue are almost always worth repairing. A 25-30 year old system leaking in multiple spots is usually cheaper to replace seamlessly than to patch in five places.

**Q: Do you handle insurance claim repairs after a storm?**
A: Yes. Post-hurricane we document damage with photos and work with insurance adjusters.

**Q: Do you repair gutters you didn't install?**
A: Yes. Most of our repair work is on systems installed by someone else.
