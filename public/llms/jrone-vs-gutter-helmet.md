## Gutter Helmet - What They Are and What They Do

Gutter Helmet is a surface-tension style gutter guard. Instead of a mesh screen, it uses a solid hood that curves over the gutter. Water follows the curve by surface tension into the gutter; debris slides off. It's a legitimate design with decades of track record - and specific weaknesses.

Gutter Helmet's strengths:
- Handles large-leaf debris effectively
- Solid-hood construction stands up to heavy impact
- Long manufacturer warranty when installed by authorized dealers

Gutter Helmet's weaknesses:
- **Struggles with pine needles.** Surface-tension designs can let pine needles track along the curve and lodge in the slot. Tampa's pine-heavy neighborhoods (and Florida in general) see this failure mode.
- **Price.** Gutter Helmet typically runs $12 - $18+ per linear foot - $2,000 - $3,500+ on an average Tampa home.
- **Sales-heavy model.** Like LeafFilter, Gutter Helmet installs through authorized dealers running traditional in-home sales pitches with financing pitches and time-pressure closes.
- **Heavy water events.** In extreme rainfall - common in Tampa summer storms - surface-tension designs can be overrun, with water spilling over the curve instead of following it into the gutter.

## Surface-Tension vs Micro Mesh - The Design Trade-off

Both designs have legitimate engineering behind them. In a Tampa-specific climate evaluation:

**Surface-tension (Gutter Helmet-style) works best when:**
- Debris is primarily large leaves
- Rainfall is moderate and steady
- Trees are deciduous with clean seasonal leaf drops

**Micro mesh (JR One-style) works best when:**
- Debris mix includes pine needles, seed pods, and fine organic matter
- Rainfall events include high-intensity bursts (afternoon thunderstorms, tropical systems)
- Trees drop year-round rather than seasonally

Tampa's profile matches the micro mesh use case. That's why JR One Aluminum defaults to recommending micro mesh for Tampa homes.
