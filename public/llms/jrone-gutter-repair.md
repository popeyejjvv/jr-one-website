## Common Gutter Problems in Tampa

### Sagging Gutters
Hangers pull away from fascia. Usually caused by:
- Waterlogged debris (uncleaned gutters) overloading hangers
- Rotted fascia no longer holding the screws
- Hangers spaced too far apart at original install
- Impact from fallen branches or storm debris

### Leaking Seams and Corners
End caps, outside corners, and inside miters leak when:
- Sealant cures, cracks, and pulls away after 10–15 years
- The gutter was rehung without re-sealing joints
- Seams from sectional gutters (non-seamless) open up over time

### Overflow / Water Cascading Over the Front
Not always a cleaning problem. Other causes:
- Gutter pitch wrong (flat or backwards — water pools and overflows)
- Undersized gutters for the roof area
- Too few downspouts for the volume
- Downspout clogged (water can't exit fast enough)
- Roof valley concentrating water at one point

### Pulling Away from Fascia
The fascia itself is the problem. Rotted fascia can't hold hangers no matter how many you add. Repair requires fascia replacement first, then gutter re-hang.

### Downspout Issues
- Clogged (leaves packed tight, needs snaking)
- Disconnected at elbow
- Crushed or dented from impact
- Dumping water at foundation instead of routing away

## When Repair Stops Making Sense

Some systems are past repair:
- Gutters 25+ years old with multiple failure points
- Sectional (non-seamless) gutters with chronic seam leaks
- Gutters that have been re-hung multiple times already
- Gutters that were installed wrong from the start (wrong size, wrong pitch, wrong hanger spacing)

JR One Aluminum will tell you honestly when the math favors replacement. A new seamless aluminum gutter system typically runs $1,500–$2,500 for a single-family home. If repairs are adding up to more than a third of that in 2–3 years of rolling patches, replacement is the better play.
